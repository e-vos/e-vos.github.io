Introduction

The term "digital divide" is usually framed as a disparity between those who have access to the internet and those who do not. According to the Pew Research Center, 96% of adults in America use the internet. Unsurprisingly, gaps in connectivity exist for differen demographic and economic groups. The people in this country who earn the least also use the internet the least. Some interesting trends exist for internet use by race and ethnicity (99% of Asian respondents and 97% of Hispanic respondents use the internet, compared with 96% and 94% for White and Black respondents, respectively). Broadly speaking, however, the poorest and most rural communities seem to have the least access.

Internet connectivity is one aspect of the digital divide. Studies like those conducted by the Pew Research Center are useful for generalizing how individuals use technology, and no doubt paint a picture representative of the average person within those groups. There exists another important, though less discussed, part of the digital divide: the disconnect between data and policy.
