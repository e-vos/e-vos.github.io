Post-fire Vegetation Response in the Sierra Nevada

2024 / STUDY
This study tests a new method for quantifying post-fire vegetation response using NASA's Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2 (HLS) project. Burn area of the 2020 North Complex Fire in California. It torched almost 500 square miles of forest over the course of 109 days.

Problem: Temporal coverage and granularity

The North Complex Fire was a major wildfire event that remained uncontained between 17 August and 3 December, 2020, straddling the eastern portion of Butte County and the southwestern portion of Plumas County in Northern California. The burn area is characterized by steep canyons, mountainous terrain, and numerous streams and small lakes, resulting in strong microclimatic and topographic gradients within and across the fire perimeter.

Forest composition within the burned area is dominated by serotinous conifers, typical of Plumas National Forest and the Northern Sierra Nevada. Post-fire regeneration in these systems is highly dynamic, with regeneration timing and rates influenced by fire severity, elevation, aspect, and interannual climate variability. As a result, vegetation recovery is neither spatially nor temporally uniform, and early regrowth processes can occur over short timescales that are difficult to resolve with sparsely sampled observations.

Solution: Pixel-based analyses of harmonized multispectral data

The problem of uneven rates of regrowth is compounded by atmospheric obstructions of the satellite sensor itself, such as clouds or smoke. In order to quantify regrowth as accurately as possible, a compromise needs to be made between the frequency and resolution of images. While the 30-meter resolution of Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2 (HLS) data is twice as coarse as Sentinel-2 alone, the combined temporal resolution of the two programs in this context compensates for it. Using HLS ensures the maximum number of usable images at a reasonable resolution.

Methodology

Extraction of the burn region can be done easily by calculating certain vegetation indices. In the above figure, I gained the burn region by filtering out certain pixel values from an NDVI raster.

While the burn region can accurately be extracted from HLS data itself, it was more convenient to use the one produced by CAL FIRE. This polygon was split into 17 sectors of roughly 18,000 acres each. A representative HLS image was downloaded to create a point grid, where each point corresponded to the center of an HLS pixel. These points were then assigned a value based on the sector they were located in.

Burn region split into equal area sectors.

HLS data was downloaded for 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2023. Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) was then calculated for each capture date. Overall NDVI value changes were calculated for each year. 2020 saw a mean NDVI change of -0.502. In 2021, the mean NDVI change was still negative overall at -0.051. A rapid increase in greenness was seen in 2022 with a mean change of 0.381. 2023 saw the strongest mean change at 0.519. Intrasector change calculations showed that great variation existed in both damage and regrowth. Sector 6 in particular, located in the western-central portion of the burn region, was found ot be both most negatively affected overall by the fire and to have exhibited the most growth overall when compared to other sectors. Sector 1, the most south-westerly sector, saw slightly negative change between 2022 and 2023, despite relatively strong regrowth occurring between 2021 and 2022.

Significance

Despite the intensity and duration of this wildfire, the process of post-fire regeneration began quite quickly (within one year) of its containment. The forests of the Sierra Nevada are not monoliths, and the complexity of species composition, terrain, weather, and many other environmental factors all contribute to the differences seen in how efficient the regeneration process is. What can ultimately be stated about the conifer forests of this area is that they are well-adapted to fire disturbance events. Studies like this cannot characterize what kind of vegetation is occurring. Certainly the conifers found in this region do not recover within a few years, but the plant species that facilitate their recovery may.
