Town of Gosnold, Massachusetts, Resilience Planning Tool

2024 / PRODUCT
This project was performed while working with the University of Rhode Island Environmental Data Center. The Elizabeth Islands, the entirety of which the Town of Gosnold encompasses.

Problem

Gosnold is the least populous municipality in Massachusetts, and is accessed by either ferry (from New Bedford) or by private boat. An archipelagic community, it lies between Buzzards Bay and Vineyard Sound. By distance, its easternmost point is a little less than 0.5 kilometers from Woods Hole (Falmouth). From New Bedford, the journey is made in about an hour. About 67 people live there, mostly on Cuttyhunk Island.

I was asked to create a replacement planning tool for the Town. This tool would derive its data from the latest LiDAR scans and aerial imagery available for Gosnold, and would add some new features lacking in the previous version.

Methodology and Significance

Several important features were derived from LiDAR data, including contour lines, digital elevation models, and slope maps. Sea-level rise scenarios from NOAA were added as well. These and other layers were integrated into a single web application that allows anyone to quickly visualize and combine various datapoints.

Some islands have high bluffs. The application is capable of showing slope values and accurately showing elevation gradients.

While not directly requested, the elevation product layers revealed some interesting ground textures on some islands. Working with someone from the Rhode Island Natural History Survey, we identified what appear to be remnants of agricultural activity (e.g. plowing, tilling). Some islands have stone wall networks, evidence of the farming legacy that existed on the archipelago. Without this analysis, these textures would remain obscured by vegetation as they likely have been for over a century.

While faint, textures like this are found in many places. These are furrows, produced by 19th century agricultural activity.
